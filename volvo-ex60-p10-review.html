Volvo EX60 P10: Beyond the 400-Mile Headline

The P10 offers a clearer picture of Volvo’s new family EV. Wheel size, measured highway range and everyday controls deserve closer attention than the biggest number in the launch campaign.

First, identify the version

A family EV is bought for hundreds of ordinary journeys. The useful question is whether it makes those journeys easy, not whether the most expensive future version wins a range comparison. With the EX60, that distinction begins with the badge: P6, P10 and P12 do not share one universal range figure.

Volvo lists up to 330 miles of EPA range for the P10 on 20-inch wheels, with U.S. deliveries scheduled for fall 2026. The widely discussed 400-mile figure belongs to the P12: it is preliminary, applies to 20-inch wheels and accompanies a 2027 delivery schedule. It is not an EPA result for the current P10. [1]

P10, 20-inch wheelsUp to 330 miles, EPA
P12, 20-inch wheelsUp to 400 miles, preliminary; deliveries in 2027
P10 manufacturer acceleration0–60 mph in 4.4 seconds
Independent test configurationP10 Ultra AWD on 22-inch wheels

The U.S. model page gives a starting MSRP of $59,795 for the range. That starting figure is not the price of a fully equipped P10. Buyers should compare the same trim, destination charges and options before treating two advertised prices as competitors. [1]

What the independent test actually tells us

MotorTrend tested a P10 Ultra AWD on 22-inch wheels. That car carried a 312-mile EPA rating and traveled 280 miles in the publication’s 70-mph highway range test. It also reached 60 mph in 3.9 seconds, quicker than Volvo’s quoted figure. These are results for that configuration and test procedure, rather than a promise for every EX60. [2]

The distinction changes the shopping conversation. A highway result measured at a fixed speed is more directly relevant to a long interstate trip than a blended certification number. Even so, a single result cannot predict a winter drive, a headwind or a route with sustained climbing. Leave room in the plan for those conditions.

It also makes the wheel choice worth discussing before delivery. Compare the range rating attached to the wheels you will actually buy. The larger set should earn its place through a benefit you value, rather than quietly becoming the default.

Charging is a session, not a peak number

Volvo advertises a 10-to-80-percent charge in as little as 16 minutes at a 400-kW station under suitable conditions. The station’s rating is not a statement that the car accepts 400 kW continuously. Battery temperature, starting charge and the charging curve affect the stop. [1]

For a family trip, the more useful comparison is time added to a complete route. That includes reaching the charger, waiting for a bay and buying enough energy for the next leg. At home, the question changes again: a reliable overnight connection can matter more than a spectacular fast-charge headline.

Autoblog’s overview is useful for tracking the EX60’s positioning and versions. For changing prices and delivery timing, this review prioritizes Volvo’s current U.S. page over earlier launch coverage. [4]

The cabin has to work every morning

Road & Track describes a quiet, refined ride but criticizes the friction involved in operating screen-based controls. Those are attributed impressions, not observations from an AutoGlob drive. [3]

A prospective owner can examine that trade-off without a test track. During a dealer demonstration, change the temperature, redirect the vents and recover the navigation screen. Try the functions in the order you use them on a rushed morning. A pleasing display should also make routine adjustments straightforward.

The P10 makes the strongest case when its actual configuration suits the household: sufficient highway margin, convenient charging and controls everyone can use. The future P12’s bigger range number does not answer those questions. The right EX60 is the one that makes the family’s real itinerary easier.
